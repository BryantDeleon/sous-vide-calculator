Before the bath

The times above assume the bag is tight against the food

Air pockets insulate food from the water, so a loosely bagged steak heats unevenly and can float to the surface. Removing the air keeps every side in contact with the bath.

  • Bags stay sunk, so food heats on schedule.
  • Marinades and butter stay pressed against the food.
  • Seal portions ahead, freeze them, and cook straight from frozen.

How the calculator works

Temperature sets doneness. Time makes it safe.

The bath temperature decides how done the food will be, because the food can never get hotter than the water. Time does two separate jobs, and the calculator adds them together.

Heat through

How long the center takes to climb from fridge temperature to within 1°F of the bath. It depends on thickness and shape, not weight. A 2-inch steak takes about four times as long as a 1-inch steak. We use Douglas Baldwin's published heating tables for slab-shaped and cylinder-shaped cuts, and add about 50% for frozen food. Fish is pulled a little earlier, within a few degrees of the bath, because its texture suffers from long cooks.

Safety hold

Below the usual "safe" temperatures, food becomes safe by being held long enough. Chicken at 140°F needs about 40 minutes at temperature to pasteurize. At 160°F it needs seconds. The hold times come from USDA FSIS lethality tables, calculated 1°F below the bath to be cautious.

Ready window

Once food is heated and safe, it won't overcook, but it can turn soft or mushy. The window shows how long it can wait in the bath. Below 130°F the total cook stays under 4 hours, because bacteria can grow at those temperatures.

Tips

Getting it right the first time

Preheat the bath first

The times start when the bag goes into water that's already at temperature. Add 10–20 minutes if your circulator is heating a large pot from cold tap water.

Keep bags fully under water

A floating bag cooks slowly on the exposed side. Remove as much air as possible, and weigh down or clip bags that still float.

Dry, then sear hard and fast

Pat the surface dry and sear in a very hot pan for under a minute per side. Long searing overcooks the outer layer you worked to keep even.

Chill fast if you're not eating now

Move the sealed bag into an ice bath (half ice, half water) until cold through, then refrigerate. Slow cooling on the counter gives bacteria time to grow.

Check your bag's temperature rating

Most bags handle meat and fish temperatures easily. For long cooks or vegetables at 183°F, use bags rated for that heat.

Season simply

Salt, pepper and a little fat are enough. Raw garlic and strong aromatics can taste harsh after long cooks, so use them sparingly or add them when searing.

FAQ

Sous vide questions, answered

How long does it take to sous vide a 1-inch steak?

A 1-inch steak takes about 1 h 20 min to heat through at any temperature, and it can stay in the bath for up to 3 h 20 min before the texture softens. For medium-rare, set the bath to 131°F (55°C). A 2-inch steak needs about 3 h 35 min, because heating time grows with the square of the thickness.

Is sous vide safe below USDA's recommended temperatures?

Yes, when the food is held at temperature long enough. Killing bacteria depends on both temperature and time. USDA's 165°F (74°C) for chicken kills Salmonella almost instantly, but chicken held at 140°F (60°C) for about 32 minutes reaches the same reduction. The calculator adds these hold times from USDA FSIS tables. Whole cuts of beef and lamb are contaminated mainly on the surface, so a sear makes them safe at lower temperatures.

How long should I sous vide chicken breast?

For a 1-inch boneless breast at 145°F (63°C), cook 1 h 35 min to 3 h 35 min. That includes a safety hold so the center is fully pasteurized. Use 140°F (60°C) for the most tender result, or 150°F (66°C) for a more familiar texture.

Can you overcook food with sous vide?

Not in terms of doneness, since food can't get hotter than the water. But texture changes with time. Steaks and chicken turn soft and mealy after several hours, and fish starts to break down after about 30 minutes past the minimum time. Each result shows a ready window so you know how long food can wait.

Can I sous vide from frozen?

Yes. Put the frozen, sealed bag straight into the preheated bath and add about 50% to the heating time. The calculator does this when you choose "From frozen". Don't thaw food at room temperature first.

Does weight or thickness matter for sous vide time?

Thickness. Heat travels into food from every side, so the time depends on the distance to the center, not how heavy the piece is. Two 8-ounce steaks of the same thickness take the same time as one. Measure the thickest point of the cut.

What temperature should I sous vide salmon?

For tender, moist salmon that just starts to flake, use 122°F (50°C) for 50 min to 1 h 20 min for a 1-inch fillet. Use 110°F (43°C) for a silky, translucent texture or 130°F (54°C) for flaky fish. Fish isn't pasteurized below 130°F, so use very fresh, previously frozen salmon.

Do I need a vacuum sealer for sous vide?

No, but it helps. You can use zipper bags and the water displacement method to push out air. A vacuum sealer removes more air, so bags don't float, food heats evenly, and you can seal and freeze portions ahead of time to cook straight from the freezer.

Why should sous vide cooks below 130°F stay under 4 hours?

Some bacteria, including Clostridium perfringens, can still grow below about 130°F (54.4°C). Food safety guidance allows unpasteurized food in that range for under 4 hours, so the calculator caps rare steaks and delicate fish there and warns you if a thick cut needs longer.
